Use this reference for reader-facing Vietnamese that feels machine-shaped, over-smoothed, translation-shaped, or mismatched to its professional register. It extends the canonical editor; it does not create another Vietnamese voice owner.

## THREE-LAYER DIAGNOSIS

Read in this order:

1. **Paragraph:** check argument flow, opening/closing function, repeated structure, abstract claims without usable information, and uniform rhythm.
2. **Sentence:** identify actor, action, logic, certainty, condition, and the information that should arrive first in natural Vietnamese.
3. **Word:** only then inspect clichés, nominalization, weak verbs, literal metaphors, filler transitions, and terminology.

When several patterns hit one sentence, repair it once at the structural level and reassess. Do not perform a chain of one-for-one synonym replacements.

## INTERVENTION LEVELS

- **Light:** local repair while preserving structure and voice.
- **Medium:** merge or split sentences, remove empty framing, or clarify the actor.
- **Deep:** reorder a paragraph only when its structure hides the decision or argument; recheck the preservation ledger after every paragraph.

Prefer the lowest level that solves the reader problem. Keep already-natural sentences unchanged.

## REGISTER MAP

- **Technical:** prioritize precision, explicit actors, consistent terms, and unchanged code/API/paths. Passive voice may be correct when the process matters more than the operator.
- **Workplace:** state action, owner, deadline, and handoff directly; preserve the organization's form of address.
- **Academic:** retain hedges, limitations, citations, terminology, and valid nominalization.
- **Marketing/product:** persuasion requires supplied evidence; never invent benefits, metrics, testimonials, or urgency.
- **Customer care:** acknowledge the issue briefly, then state current status and next action; do not promise beyond authority.
- **Social/blog:** preserve authentic colloquial, regional, generational, and rhythmic choices when they belong to the source voice.
- **Administrative/legal/policy:** precision outranks conversational ease; preserve definitions, scope, conditions, obligation words, and legally functional lists.

## TRANSLATIONESE PATTERNS

- Long noun chains, stacked nominalization, dummy subjects, weak verb constructions, English-order passive clauses, literal business metaphors, and multiple administrative layers may justify structural repair.
- `thông qua việc`, passive voice, long sentences, Sino-Vietnamese terms, `tận dụng`, and `liền mạch` are not errors by themselves.
- Preserve near-English order when subtitle timing, bilingual forms, or sentence alignment requires it.
- Do not convert `may` into certainty, `shall` into advice, association into causation, or an approved term into a newly invented Vietnamese term.

## TWO-WAY AUDIT

1. Compare output against input to detect missing facts, numbers, names, dates, negation, conditions, exceptions, certainty, scope, quotations, and terms.
2. Read output alone to detect awkward rhythm, register drift, illogical transitions, unnecessary edits, and terminology inconsistency.

Any invented fact or changed decision truth is a blocker even when the result sounds fluent. When naturalness conflicts with preservation, preserve truth and flag the unresolved wording.
